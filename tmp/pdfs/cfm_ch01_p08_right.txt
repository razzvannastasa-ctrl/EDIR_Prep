                                             CHAPTER 1: THE ORGANISATION OF WORLD FOOTBALL



The European Football Championship is UEFA’s competition for senior men’s national teams and is one of
the biggest sporting events in the world (UEFA.com, n.d. (c)). The first edition was contested between 1958
and 1960 (as the European Nations’ Cup), with the Soviet Union beating Yugoslavia 2-1 in the final. The
2016 finals took place in France, where the 16-team format was replaced by a 24-team tournament, with
Portugal emerging victorious.

Exceptionally, EURO 2020 will have a special one-off format to celebrate the championship’s 60th anniversary.
Styled as a ‘EURO for Europe’, the final tournament will be hosted by 12 national associations in 12 different
cities across Europe, with the semi-finals and the final being held at London’s Wembley Stadium. As a result
of the COVID-19 pandemic, it will take place in summer 2021.

EURO 2016 was one of the most successful sporting tournaments ever staged, not least in terms of
social media interest. There were more than 300 million visits to EURO2016.com and the official apps
during the tournament, generating 1.5 billion page impressions between 9 June (the day before the
opening match) and 11 July (the day after the final) – almost four times the levels seen during EURO 2012
(UEFA.com, 12 July 2016). EURO 2016 generated a total of €1.92bn in revenue, an increase of 38% on 2012
(UEFA, 2017b, p. 23).

As the success of EURO 2016 illustrates, there is huge fan interest in European national team competitions.
However, interest in national team friendlies is in decline across all stakeholder groups – among supporters,
clubs, players, broadcasters and sponsors. Friendly matches tend to lack competitive edge, and broadcasters
and sponsors prefer to partner with season-long competitions, rather than individual games.

In 2018, in response to that trend, UEFA launched a new league-based competition, with promotion and
relegation between four tiers of closely matched national teams: the UEFA Nations League. Portugal were
crowned champions in the inaugural final against the Netherlands in June 2019. As well as being a stand-
alone competition in its own right, it is an alternative qualification route for UEFA EUROs and FIFA World
Cups. The structure of the competition ensures a high level of competitive balance between the participating
teams and, because media rights are sold collectively by UEFA, the majority of the participating teams are
guaranteed higher revenues than they could have achieved through individual bargaining with broadcasters
and sponsors for friendly matches (UEFA.com, n.d. (d)).

The Champions League is UEFA’s main competition for club teams and the most successful club competition
in any sport anywhere in the world (UEFA.com, n.d. (e)). It was originally called the European Champion
Clubs’ Cup and was launched in its original form one month after the first UEFA Congress in 1955, having
been instigated by French sports newspaper L’Équipe. Initially, participation was by invitation only, but it was
later opened up to the league champions of all UEFA member associations. In 1992, the competition was
rebranded the UEFA Champions League. For the 1992/93 season, a group stage was introduced, with the
top two teams from each group qualifying for a 16-team knockout stage. The competition format has proved
to be a phenomenal success, and 32 teams now compete in the group stage each season. The Champions
League is organised in three-season cycles, with the current cycle running from 2018/19 to 2020/21.
