HANDBOOK OF FOOTBALL ASSOCIATION MANAGEMENT



A standard element in any business strategy process is the concept of ‘stakeholder mapping’ (Whittington et al.,
2019, pp. 396–401). The idea is that successful organisations must constantly monitor their environment
and adjust their strategies to match the changing stakeholders, both internally and externally.

UEFA’s stakeholders can be seen in Figure 1.1 (adapted from UEFA, 2005, p. 30). What this diagram demonstrates
is that the range of active stakeholders in the football ecosystem goes well beyond industry actors such as
FIFA, national associations, domestic leagues and clubs; it also incorporates sporting partners such as the
European governing bodies of other team sports and the Olympic movement, as well as external partners
such as business and government.

As stated above, the primary purpose of this chapter is to explain how world football is organised. The
ecosystem metaphor is particularly powerful here because the degree of interdependence between key
stakeholders is much higher in the sports ecosystem than in most other sectors of the economy. This is
because globalisation has introduced new and often unpredictable forces and stakeholders into the sports
ecosystem and their impact needs to be constantly evaluated through strategic analysis. Effective policies
can then be developed in response.
