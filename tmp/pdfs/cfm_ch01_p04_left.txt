HANDBOOK OF FOOTBALL ASSOCIATION MANAGEMENT



> 2.1. FIFA and the Olympic movement

Football is integrated into the wider global sports community by virtue of the fact that it is listed in the
Olympic Charter (Olympic.org, 2020) as a recognised Olympic sport, and FIFA is one of the international sports
federations that comprises the Olympic movement. Since 1992, most players in the men’s Olympic football
competition have had to be under 23 years of age, with only three players over the age of 23 allowed per
squad, so the tournament is now effectively an Under-23 World Cup.

> 2.2. FIFA as a competition organiser, developer and promoter of football globally

FIFA is perhaps best known for the competitions it runs, notably the FIFA World Cup, which is one of the
biggest sporting events in the world. The 2018 tournament was held in Russia and won by France, and the
2022 FIFA World Cup will take place in Qatar. The tournament’s global significance can be seen in the fact
that the 2018 FIFA World Cup reached 3.57 billion viewers (FIFA.com, 21 December 2018).

The 2018 World Cup generated total income of $5.36bn for FIFA (FIFA, 2020, p. 34), derived predominantly
from the sale of broadcasting and sponsorship rights, but the tournament also enjoyed very high stadium
attendance figures, with crowds averaging 98.2% of stadium capacity (FIFA, 2020, p. 34).

The event is also a huge money-spinner, generating a profit (i.e. revenue minus expenses) of $3.53bn in
2018 (FIFA, 2020, p. 34), a surplus used to finance FIFA’s wider activities over the four-year cycle between
World Cups. FIFA’s total revenue over the 2015–18 period was $6.42bn (FIFA, 2020, p. 17), which shows
the extent of FIFA’s commercial rights exploitation activities.
