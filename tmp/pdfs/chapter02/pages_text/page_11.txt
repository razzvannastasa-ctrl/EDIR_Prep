	> 3.3.2. Analysis of strengths and weaknesses
Strategies should increase the likelihood of success by leveraging areas where the national association is 
strong and strengthening areas where it is weak. Internal analysis helps the organisation to determine where 
its strengths and weaknesses lie. One way of objectively doing this is to score and rank all resources and 
capabilities on the basis of both their importance and their relative strength and then map them on a grid. 
Importance is linked to how valuable a given resource is for success (usually following a KSF analysis). As 
there is no scientific way of scoring importance, it is recommended that more than one person provide an 
assessment, in order to produce a mathematical average or a mutually agreed score.
Relative strength indicates how well the organisation performs when it comes to a given resource or capability. 
If the scoring is on a scale of 1 to 10, a score of 5 would indicate that the organisation is about average with 
regard to that particular resource or capability. Unlike the scoring for importance, the scoring for relative 
strength may be based on scientific methods, and whenever measurable indicators are available, scores can 
be calculated by comparing those indicators with equivalent data for similar national associations.

The strengths and weaknesses grid comprises four quadrants: 
•	Key strengths: areas where the national association has valuable resources and capabilities that 
are unique or where it outscores other associations.
•	Key weaknesses: areas that are important for the success of the national association, but where 
it is not performing as well as it should.
•	Superfluous strengths: areas where the national association is strong, but which are largely 
irrelevant to its success.
•	Irrelevant weaknesses: areas where the national association is weak, but which are also largely 
irrelevant to its success.
Understanding strengths and weaknesses is extremely important when assessing strategy 
robustness, as strategies that rely on areas where the national association is generally weak 
are less likely to be successfully carried out.
