The planning process typically takes place over three calendar months. Some associations present updates 
to their strategic plans at their general meetings, while others do so at the end of the competition season.
In the example in Figure 2.2, the process is initiated in June. On the basis of the previous year’s performance 
and changes to the internal and external environment (box 1), the general secretary or a member of their 
staff analyses the situation and presents the findings at a strategic planning review meeting (box 2), where 
the information is discussed and general guidelines for updates to the strategic plan are decided. These 
guidelines are then formalised in proposed updates (box 3), to be presented to and approved by the executive 
committee, with a follow-up a couple of months later (box 4). Implementation schemes and budgets are 
then decided on this basis.
Depending on the type of strategic management system the organisation adopts, this process can be 
performed every three to five years, with updates every year.

STRATEGIC MANAGEMENT IN PRACTICE: 
THE UEFA GROW STRATEGY DEVELOPMENT PROCESS
In July 2020, UEFA Grow published its own framework for strategic planning as part of the Football 
Federations of the Future initiative. The framework, which is itself a strategic management system, 
contains a number of practical suggestions for how national associations can approach strategic 
planning. It suggests an eight-step process to develop a strategy from scratch over a four-to-six-month 
period. The main steps and timing are depicted below and the full framework can be found on UEFA 
Academy Online, the online platform set up by UEFA for sharing football resources.
