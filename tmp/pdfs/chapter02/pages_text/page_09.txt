The identification and mapping of the domains in which the association is active is sometimes a sufficient 
starting point for developing a strategy. As will be described later in this chapter, goals, objectives and strategies 
must be formulated and developed for each domain. Management may, however, decide to focus more 
on some domains than others. Information on expected results, costs, investment and revenue will all help 
with this decision and therefore help to produce coherent strategic management. Where the dynamics of a 
specific domain are not clearly understood, analysis can be carried out in order to enhance understanding. 
Two such analytical methods (key success factors and PEST analysis) are described below.
	> 3.2.2. Understanding key success factors
Key success factors (KSFs) are elements within a specific domain that determine how an organisation can 
be successful, i.e. achieve its goals.
KSFs are identified by answering two simple questions:
1.	What drives and determines stakeholders’ behaviour in this domain?
2.	How can our national association meet stakeholders’ expectations in this domain?
A variation on the second question is:
2a.	How can our national association meet stakeholders’ expectations better than other organisations?
Trying to understand the KSFs in each of an association’s domains is a very interesting exercise and can in 
many cases improve the association’s ability to obtain results, since its strategies become easier to formulate 
and therefore more effective. Table 2.5 provides an example of KSF analysis in a specific domain.

	> 3.2.3. PEST analysis
Another way to gain a better understanding of a national association’s domains is to analyse political, 
economic, social and technological (PEST) trends.
Under this framework, each domain is examined for its exposure to changes in PEST trends. In the above 
example of youth development, an ageing population and people having children later in life might change 
the KSFs and require adaptation of the sport and communication between clubs and parents. In the same 
vein, advances in technology, the explosive growth of social media and the emergence of new social media 
platforms also change KSFs and need to be responded to.
